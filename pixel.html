Your Leads Got Cheaper And Worse At The Same Time. That Is Not A Coincidence — It Is The System Working Exactly As Instructed.

You told me the first campaign averaged around £35 a lead and it was good. This one averages £10 and it is full of time wasters.

Most people would read that as bad luck, or tired creative. We think it is the most important number in your business right now, because it points at something structural.

Cheaper leads are not a discount. They are a different kind of person.

What Meta Is Actually Being Told To Do
What Meta can currently see: Someone submitted the form YES What Meta cannot currently see: Did they answer the phone? no Did they agree to a survey? no Did you drive out to them? no Did it turn into an install? no Was it another installer price-checking you? no
This is the whole problem in one line

Meta is optimising for the only outcome it can measure: a submitted form. Nothing about what happens after that form ever gets back to it.

So when you tell it to go and find more of what works, it has no idea that four of those eighty became jobs. As far as the algorithm is concerned, all eighty were identical successes. It then goes looking for more people like all eighty — and because it is also trying to be efficient, it goes looking for the cheapest ones.

The cheapest person to get a form out of is the person with the least intent. The man on a night shift who is bored. The installer checking your panel prices. They cost less to reach precisely because they are not going to buy anything.

The system is not broken. It is doing exactly what it has been told to do, very efficiently.

Why The Price Per Lead Falling Is The Warning Light
First campaign ~£35 per lead "we were snowballed with people that wanted to go ahead" This campaign ~£10 per lead 50/50 at best, 20% answer the phone, 4 jobs from 80
Your own numbers, and they tell the story better than anything we could have found

Between those two campaigns the cost per lead fell by roughly two thirds and the quality collapsed. Your agency reported the first half of that as a win. It is the symptom.

Here is what we think happened. A new campaign resets what Meta has learned — it starts again from nothing. Given a fresh start and only "form submitted" to aim at, it will find the cheapest available form-filler. The first campaign had run long enough to drift towards better people almost by accident. The restart threw that away.

Which means the new creative coming out this week is unlikely to fix it on its own. Better video into the same instruction produces better-looking ads pointed at the same wrong audience. We would genuinely like to be wrong about that, and next week you will know.

We want to be careful here: we have not seen inside your ad account. This is our read of what you described. Confirming it takes ten minutes in Ads Manager, and we will do it with you.

And a point that matters more than it first looks

Your video ads do not go to your website. You said it yourself — the ad goes to a form, and your landing pages are for the website, not the ads. So this is a separate hole from the one above, not the same one.

What it costs you: every person who lands on your site from Google, from your SEO, from a van, from a referral checking you out — is invisible. You cannot retarget any of them, and you cannot tell which of your twenty-six town pages has ever produced an enquiry. You are paying £750 a month for SEO and there is no instrument attached to it.

There is also no thank-you page. That matters for a specific reason: a thank-you URL is the normal way to tell any ad platform "this one completed". Without one, even a correctly installed pixel would have nothing to fire on.

What Fixing It Actually Means
  • Feed the real outcome back to Meta, not the form. When someone answers the phone, books a survey or signs, that fact goes back into the ad platform. This is the one that changes what kind of person arrives, because it finally changes what the algorithm is aiming at.
  • Put the ads onto a page you own, with a pixel on it. At the moment the most valuable data in your business is generated on someone else's form, and you do not keep it. If you ever change agency, you leave with nothing.
  • Give the site the basics it has never had — analytics, a thank-you page, call tracking against a trackable number. Until that exists, nobody can tell you honestly whether your SEO spend is working, including the people currently telling you it is not.
  • Score the leads before you drive to them. You already collect usage and property details. That is enough to rank them, so your survey days go to the ones worth surveying.
  • Then, and only then, put more money into it. Doubling spend against the current setup doubles the time wasters too. You have seen that happen already — last time you spent roughly double and got more good jobs and more time wasters in the same proportion.

Nothing here is a criticism of your ad company's creative. The filming is good and the reach is real.

The gap is that nothing downstream of the form is being measured, so nothing downstream of the form can be improved. That is fixable, and it is the difference between £10 leads and £35 ones.
