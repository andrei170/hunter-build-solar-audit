The Short Version
  • Your SEO is not the problem and we will say so on the call. You have 26 town-and-service pages across seven towns — Bury St Edmunds, Ipswich, Colchester, Norwich, Thetford, Diss and Stowmarket. That is better structure than any nearby competitor we could measure.
  • You are spending roughly £3,700 a month across two agencies — £3,000 on social (£1,200 management + £1,800 spend) and £700 on SEO. In the last six weeks the social spend produced 80 leads, of which 4 were real. That is ~£1,125 per real lead, and your own words for the rest were “no intention of having solar.”
  • But that channel has turned over £800,000. It is not a failed channel and this is not a case for firing anyone — it has degraded, and the question is when and why.
  • You filmed the creative eight weeks ago. The collapse covers the last six. Worth asking your agency what the frequency has been and when the last asset was shot — volume has held while intent has gone, which is what creative fatigue looks like. Raised as a question, not a conclusion.
  • A long form that does not qualify is the worst of both worlds. Length is not qualification — if it asked ownership, roof size and timing, it would separate a 12-panel enquiry from an 80-panel one before you drive anywhere.
  • Five jobs a week may not be a lead problem. At 1–1.5 days of surveying per job, five jobs is 5–7.5 days of surveying in a five-day week. If only you can survey, more leads will not produce more jobs.
  • Your SEO is two months old, not established — and it is your cheapest channel. £700/mo producing 10–12 leads, against £3,000/mo producing 4 real ones in six weeks. Search leads arrive with intent by definition.
  • There is not one tracking tag on any of them. No GA4, no Tag Manager, no Google Ads tag, no pixel, no call tracking.
  • So nobody can tell you how many of your twenty weekly enquiries came from the lead platform and how many came from the 26 pages you also pay for. By default the platform gets credit for both — which is comfortable for them and expensive for you.
  • Twenty enquiries produce three or four jobs. You are not short of enquiries. Sixteen conversations a week are ending in nothing, and a lead platform cannot tell a large job from a small one — you said you prefer the larger ones.
  • Companies House registers you as 41201 — Construction of commercial buildings, trading 19 years, previously HUNTER BUILDING DEVELOPMENTS LIMITED. Your website never mentions building work once — on a 4,157-word homepage, for a company called Hunter Build & Solar. Homepage emphasis runs "home" 19 times against "business" twice, and the meta description opens with "domestic solar panels." The largest thing you do is the thing you never advertise.
  • You already publish seven commercial solar pages. They are your highest-value asset and you have no way of knowing whether a single enquiry has ever come from one.
  • We verified your MCS on the register: NAP-74463, NAPIT, solar PV and battery. It is real and current. But your register entry has no website link — while 69 of the 113 solar installers within 45 miles do. One email to NAPIT, free, this week.
  • Your numbers do not match either. The register lists 01284 765263; your site publishes only the mobile 07944 939438.
  • Smaller items: no LocalBusiness structured data (two of three nearby rivals have it), and the blog has not been touched since February.
  • The Meta Ad Library refused every request we made today (403 direct, CAPTCHA via proxy), so nothing we produced claims anything about ads anyone is running.
