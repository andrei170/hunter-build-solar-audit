Plan Of Action · Hunter Build & Solar Ltd · Bury St Edmunds, Suffolk

Fix The Bucket, Then Fill It

Prepared for Jason Hunter · 1 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
The Principle Everything Below Follows
Sequence matters more than tactics here

On the call we used a bucket. Traffic is the water. The tracking, the qualifying and the follow-up are the sides of the bucket. Right now the sides have holes in them, so pouring in more water — more ad spend — moves the problem rather than solving it. You proved this yourself: last time you spent roughly double and got “a lot more jobs that qualified, but a lot more tyre kickers again as well.”

So the order below is not a preference. Phase 0 has to happen before any spend decision is worth making, because until it does, nobody — us, your ad company, or you — can prove which channel produced which job. Everything after that is optional and sequenced by what it returns.

Nothing here is creative work. It is the instrumentation that makes every later decision an evidenced one rather than an argument between suppliers.

What Phase 0 alone changes

Within a fortnight you can answer a question you cannot answer today: of the 10–12 enquiries a month your £700 SEO produces, and the 80 a month your £3,000 social produces, which ones became the three installs you did this week?

That one answer determines whether the right move is more spend, different spend, or less spend. It is also the cheapest thing on this page.

Phase 1 — Stop Paying To Speak To The Wrong People · Weeks 1–2
1.1 — A landing page between the ad and the form

At the moment the ad links straight to a form. There is nothing in between that explains who you are, what you do, what it costs roughly, and — critically — who this is not for. So the form is the first and only filter, and a form cannot filter.

What goes on it:

  • A short video from you. Not a produced ad — you, explaining what happens next and what you need from them. You are MCS certified, 19 years trading, and you survey personally. That is the pitch.
  • Objection videos. The four we hear constantly, and which your buyers are definitely thinking: “I've been burned before”, “how do I know the quote is honest”, “how long until it pays for itself”, “what happens if something goes wrong in five years”.
  • Real installs, not stock photography. You have been filming for eight months.
  • A plain statement of who it is not for. The single most effective line on any page like this. If it says you work on properties the customer owns, with a roof of a certain size, and that a typical job is £10,000–£18,000, the bored night-shift form-filler leaves. That is the entire point.

⚠️ This will reduce your lead count and that is the intended outcome. Expect your ad company's report to show a worse cost-per-lead. That number was always the wrong one — your own words.

1.2 — Rebuild the form so it qualifies rather than just being long

Your form is already long. Length is not qualification. Replace or add the fields that separate a 12-panel enquiry from an 80-panel one:

FieldWhat it filters out
Do you own the property?Tenants, who cannot buy, no matter how interested.
Roughly how large is the roof / how many bedrooms?Lets you size the job before driving.
Roughly what is your current electricity bill?The strongest single predictor of whether solar makes financial sense for them.
When are you looking to have this done?Separates "this quarter" from "one day, maybe".
Upload a recent bill, or your half-hourly usage data — optional⭐ The most powerful filter on the list. Almost nobody without genuine intent will go and find a bill. Anyone who does is close to buying.
Are you an installer or trade?Will not stop all of them, but it makes the six-a-week price-checkers declare themselves or lie on a form, which is a different conversation.
1.3 — Score the leads one to four, and work the fours first

With those fields, every enquiry gets a score automatically. Four = owns the property, large bill, wants it this quarter. One = rents, no bill, "just curious".

You stop working the list in arrival order. Your mornings go to fours and threes. Ones and twos get an automated email sequence and nothing else — no driving, no survey. Given that surveying is your genuine bottleneck at a day to a day and a half per job, this is where your week gets handed back to you.

1.4 — Speed to lead, and the overnight problem

You already call within five to ten minutes in working hours, which is genuinely good and better than most. The gap is overnight. Leads arriving after 7pm sit until morning and go cold.

Two fixes, neither involving an AI voice — and to be explicit, nothing here puts a robot on the phone to your customers:

  • An automatic text within 60 seconds, from your number, in your words. Not a call. Something like: "Thanks for the enquiry — it's Jason at Hunter Build & Solar. I'm not calling this late, but I'll ring you tomorrow. If there's a better time, reply here." It holds the lead and sets the callback.
  • Day-parting the ads so less budget runs at 1am. Your ad company controls this — you were told “they control all that” — so it is a request to make, not something to configure yourself.
1.5 — Replace the manual log with a CRM

You track follow-up on paper, chase for two or three days, then stop. Everything before that disappears. A basic CRM gives you an automatic sequence across call, text and email over about two weeks rather than three days, a permanent record of every enquiry, and — most valuably — a reactivation list. See campaign 9.

Phase 2 — Decide What The Business Sells · Weeks 2–3
The incongruence costs you the largest jobs

Companies House has you as 41201 — construction of commercial buildings, 19 years trading, formerly Hunter Building Developments Ltd. Your homepage runs 4,157 words and says “home” 19 times against “business” twice. The meta description opens with “domestic solar panels”.

The largest thing you do is the thing you never advertise. You told us you are looking at commercial, and you are already being offered commercial leads at £160–£300 each by a portal.

The fix is a split, not a rewrite — and you already publish seven commercial solar pages:

  • Domestic stays exactly as it is. It works and it ranks.
  • Commercial gets its own entrance — its own landing page, its own form asking building type, floor area and half-hourly data, its own phone number so it is tracked separately.
  • The building history gets used. Nineteen years of construction, and roofs are your trade. For a commercial buyer, “we are builders who do solar” beats “we are a solar company” comfortably.

Phase 3 — The Campaigns · Week 3 Onward

Eleven campaigns, ordered by what they return against what they cost to stand up. Numbers 1, 2, 5 and 9 are where we would start — 9 costs nothing and uses leads you have already paid for.

⭐ 1 — Google Ads, commercial solar · the open goal

We checked the three nearest competitors we could find — Seed Renewables, SunSmart UK and BlackTech Ltd, none of whom you had heard of. None of them is running a Google Ads tag. Nobody local is bidding.

This matters more than it sounds. A person typing “commercial solar installer Suffolk” into Google is not bored at 2am. They have a building, a bill and a reason. It is the exact opposite of the traffic currently filling your form.

Start small and exact-match: commercial solar installer + your towns, business solar panels, warehouse / factory / farm solar, commercial solar Suffolk / Norfolk / Cambridgeshire. Point it at the Phase 2 commercial page, not the homepage.

⚠️ Honest caveat: no competition can also mean no search volume in a given month. The first fortnight is a volume test, not a performance test — and it is cheap to run.

⭐ 2 — Google Ads, domestic high-intent

The same logic, smaller tickets, more volume. “Solar panel installers near me”, “solar panels [town]”, “MCS certified installer [town]”, “solar battery installation”.

Two reasons it is worth running alongside the social: it captures demand rather than creating it, so answer rates are structurally higher than interruption ads; and it directly tests the claim that your SEO spend is wasted. If paid search converts well on the same terms you rank for organically, the organic traffic is worth more than the report suggested.

Also run a small brand-defence campaign on “Hunter Build and Solar”. Pennies, and it stops a competitor appearing above you when someone searches your name after seeing an ad.

3 — Restructure the Meta campaign around a deeper event

Not a replacement for Think Grow Media — a request to them. Stop optimising for form submissions. Optimise for a deeper event: a qualified submission, or a booked survey. That changes who Meta goes looking for.

Alongside it: day-parting, excluding audiences that look like the non-answerers, and a fresh conversion window on the reinstalled pixel. This single change addresses the 50/50 ratio at its root, because the ratio is what the pixel was taught to produce.

4 — Creative cadence

“A light that shines twice as bright burns half as long.” The decline covers six weeks; the creative was eight weeks old. A new asset every 7–10 days, not a large shoot every two months.

The Corfu batch should produce a lot of short cuts rather than a few polished films. For scale: a roofing company we work with runs 96 live creatives. You do not need 96 — but one batch every two months is the thing causing the pattern you described, where it works brilliantly then fades.

⭐ 5 — Retarget the sixty-four who never answered

Sixty-four people in six weeks gave you their details and then did not pick up the phone. You have already paid for every one of them. Some were time-wasters. Some were at work, or nervous, or had a partner to convince.

Build a custom audience of form-fillers who never became customers and run trust content at them, not offers: the objection videos, a real install from their town, you explaining what a survey involves and that it costs nothing. The cheapest audience you will ever advertise to.

6 — The 80-panel campaign

Your largest residential job was 80 panels, roof plus ground mount. Nothing in your marketing says you do work at that scale, so you are shown to people imagining a 12-panel terrace.

A campaign built entirely around that one install — the scale, the ground mount, the bill before and after — targeted at large detached and rural properties, raises the average job size without increasing lead volume at all. At £2,500–£3,500 profit a job, a bigger average job is worth more than a bigger list.

7 — Agricultural and rural commercial

Suffolk, Norfolk and Cambridgeshire are farm country. Barns and sheds have large, simple, south-facing roofs with no planning complications, and agricultural businesses have genuine daytime load — the best possible case for solar economics.

Reachable through the farming press, county shows and NFU channels rather than Facebook interest targeting. Slower to build, and the job sizes are the largest available to you.

8 — LinkedIn for commercial

You are already paying Think Grow Media to post there. Commercial solar is a considered B2B purchase, so LinkedIn fits it in a way it does not fit domestic.

Target facilities managers, operations directors and owners of SMEs with premises inside your radius. ⛔ We would not recommend Meta for commercial at all — wrong audience, wrong mindset.

⭐ 9 — Database reactivation · costs nothing

Every enquiry since January that never converted, and every customer who bought panels but not a battery. A short, polite sequence over a fortnight — not a blast.

Two angles that are genuinely current: battery retrofit to people who already have your panels (no acquisition cost, no roof survey, you have already built trust), and anyone who said “not right now” six months ago, for whom right now may have arrived.

⚠️ This needs the CRM from Phase 1.5 to exist first, because the records currently live in a manual log. It is the highest-return item on this page and the cheapest, and it is blocked until that is in place.

10 — Formalise the referrals

Referral is already 20% of your jobs with no system behind it whatsoever. A written referral offer to past customers, a review request sent automatically after every install, and a simple reward turns an accident into a channel.

Referred customers also answer the phone, which is the number this entire document is about.

11 — The "been burned" campaign

You paid £12,000 to a company that vanished to Dubai. Your customers carry the same fear about solar installers — the industry has deserved it, and they have read the stories.

Content that meets it head on: what MCS certification actually guarantees and how to check it on the register yourself, what your workmanship warranty covers, what happens if a panel fails in year seven, and how to read a solar quote so you can tell whether it is honest.

It converts the nervous. It also disqualifies nobody you wanted, which is rare.
