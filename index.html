First, the thing nobody should lose sight of

That social channel has turned over £800,000. It is not a failed channel and whoever runs it is not a bad agency. Any conversation that starts with “fire them” is the wrong conversation.

What has happened is that it has degraded, and the interesting question is when, and why.

Finding A — Eighty Leads, Four Real Ones
Leads delivered, last 6 weeks 80 Real ones 4 = 5% Cost of those 6 weeks ~£4,500 (1.5 x £3,000) Per real lead ~£1,125
Your words: they have “no intention of having solar”

That sentence is the whole finding. These are not badly-handled leads. They are people who were never buying anything. Ninety-five percent of what you are paying for is not a conversion problem, a follow-up problem or a salesmanship problem — it is a targeting problem upstream of all of that.

And it costs you twice. Once in the £3,000, and again in the hours spent ringing seventy-six people who were never going to buy — when you also told us a single survey takes you a day and a half.

Finding B — The Filming Was Eight Weeks Ago. The Collapse Was Six.
We are raising this as a question, not a conclusion

You filmed the creative eight weeks ago. The eighty-leads-to-four number covers the last six weeks.

Those two facts may be unrelated. But the sequence is worth asking about, because on Meta and Instagram the same creative shown repeatedly to the same local audience tends to get cheaper and worse at the same time — the people most likely to buy see it first and act, and what remains is progressively less interested. The cost per lead can look stable while the quality of the lead falls through the floor.

That is consistent with what you are describing: the volume has held at roughly twenty a week while the intent has gone.

The question for your agency is simple and fair: what has the frequency been on this creative over the last six weeks, and when was the last new asset shot? If the answer is that the same films have run since the shoot, you have probably found it. If not, the cause is elsewhere and worth finding.

Finding C — A Long Form That Does Not Qualify Is The Worst Of Both Worlds
You told us the form is a funnel and people have to put a lot of information into it

A long form should be filtering these people out. That is what length is for — somebody with no intention of buying solar does not fill in eight fields.

So if you have a long form and timewasters at the same time, the form is asking the wrong questions. Length is not qualification. A form can take four minutes and still never ask the three things that decide whether this is a job.

For your business those three are probably: do you own the property · roughly how big is the roof, or how many panels are you imagining · are you looking to do this now or researching for later. None of those is intrusive, and together they separate a 12-panel enquiry from an 80-panel one before you drive anywhere.

It is also worth knowing what the ad promises before the form. If the creative leads with savings, grants or anything free, it will deliver exactly the audience you are describing, however long the form is.

Finding D — Five Jobs A Week May Not Be A Lead Problem At All
Survey time, per job 1 to 1.5 days At 3 jobs a week 3 to 4.5 days of surveying At 5 jobs a week 5 to 7.5 days of surveying Days in a working week 5
This is the number we would want to test before spending anything on more leads

At five jobs a week, surveying alone fills or overruns the entire week — before installation, before quoting, and before the phone calls to people who were never buying.

So the honest question is not how to get more leads. It is whether the survey is the thing holding you at three, and whether anybody other than you can carry one out.

If only you can survey, more leads will not produce more jobs. They will produce a longer queue and a slower response, and the conversion rate will fall. That is worth establishing before another pound goes into advertising — and it is the sort of question an agency selling you leads has no reason to ask.

We may be wrong about this. A survey may be an hour on site and a day of desk work somebody else could do. But if it is a day and a half of your time, it is the ceiling, and no amount of marketing moves it.

Finding E — Your Newest Channel Is Your Cheapest
SEO £700/mo ~2 months old 10–12 leads Social £3,000/mo running longer ~80 leads/month, 4 real in 6 weeks
And this changes what we wrote before we spoke

We said earlier that your SEO looked like solid, established work. It is better than that — it is about two months old. Twenty-six town-and-service pages built in roughly eight weeks, already producing ten to twelve leads, for £700 a month.

Search leads arrive with intent by definition. Nobody types “commercial solar panels Thetford” with no intention of having solar. That is the structural difference between your two channels, and it is why the newer and cheaper one is worth protecting.

It is also why the tracking matters so much. You cannot currently prove which of your fifteen weekly leads came from the £3,000 and which came from the £700 — and on these numbers that is the single most valuable fact in your business.

Finding 1 — Nobody Can Prove Any Of It Works
Tracking installed on hunterbuildandsolar.co.uk: Google Analytics 4 not found Google Tag Manager not found Google Ads tag not found Meta pixel not found Call tracking not found Microsoft Clarity not found Nothing. On any page.
This is the single most expensive gap in your business right now

You told Ramses you get around twenty enquiries a week, mostly through a lead-generation platform, and that you also have somebody working on your SEO.

So here is a question nobody can currently answer: of those twenty, how many came from the platform you pay for, and how many came from the 26 pages you also pay for?

Without analytics, the platform gets the credit for everything — including the enquiries your own website earned. That is a very comfortable position for a lead platform and a very expensive one for you.

And your SEO guy is working blind too. He has built 26 pages and cannot tell you which three produce work and which twenty produce nothing. He deserves the data as much as you do.

Finding 2 — Twenty Enquiries, Three Or Four Jobs
Leads per week, all channels 15 Jobs per week 3 — you want 5 Roughly 20% convert You told us you prefer LARGER jobs. You publish 7 commercial solar pages.
A lead platform cannot tell a big job from a small one

You are not short of enquiries. You are short of the right enquiries. Sixteen or seventeen conversations a week are ending in nothing, and each one still costs you the time to answer the phone, quote it and follow it up.

A platform like the one you are using sells the same enquiry to several installers and optimises for volume, because volume is what it is paid for. It has no idea you would rather have one commercial roof than four domestic ones.

Your own website can be told that. Your seven commercial pages are the highest-value thing you publish, and you have no way of knowing whether a single enquiry has ever come from one of them.

Get the meter on, and within a month the question stops being “how do I get more leads” and becomes “how do I get more of the fourteen that came from the commercial pages.”

The word “Build” is in your company name and nowhere on your website

You are registered with Companies House as a constructor of commercial buildings, and have been trading for nineteen years. Your meta description opens with “domestic solar panels.”

Your website does not mention building work, construction, extensions or renovation anywhere — not once, on a 4,157-word homepage, for a company called Hunter Build & Solar.

You told Ramses you prefer larger jobs. The largest thing this business does is the thing it is registered to do and never advertises.

So the twenty enquiries a week are exactly what you have asked for. A consumer lead platform, a domestic-led meta description and a site weighted 19-to-2 toward homeowners will produce domestic solar enquiries — and that is what it is producing.

We are not saying drop the domestic work. We are saying the highest-value half of this business is currently invisible, and it is the half you said you want more of.

And one small thing we noticed while counting

One of your internal links reads “commercial solar panel installation in Theftord” — Thetford, misspelled. Worth passing to whoever maintains the pages.

Your MCS is current and verifiable. On a grant-backed or scheme-backed job that is the thing that lets the customer proceed at all.

But your register entry has no website on it
Your website field on the register: EMPTY Solar MCS installers within ~45 miles of you: 113 How many put a website on their entry: 69 (61%) How many did not: 44 — including you

MCS runs its own find-an-installer search, and it is exactly what a homeowner researching a grant is told to use. Sixty-nine of your local competitors put a clickable link on theirs. You are findable there and then there is nowhere to go.

This is free. It is one email to NAPIT.

And the number does not match either. The register lists 01284 765263. Your website publishes only the mobile 07944 939438. Someone who finds you through MCS rings a different number from everyone who finds you through Google — which is another enquiry source nobody is counting.

Finding 4 — Two Smaller Things Worth Fixing
Structured data found: Article · Person · Organization · WebPage · WebSite Structured data missing: LocalBusiness Service FAQPage Blog posts published: 4 Last touched: 22 February 2026 (seven months ago)
Neither is urgent, both are cheap

No LocalBusiness markup. Two of the three nearest competitors we checked have it. It is the code that tells Google you are a real business at a real address serving a real area, and it is a one-off job.

The blog stopped in February. Four posts, all last touched on the same day. That is not a crisis — your service pages are doing the heavy lifting — but it means the site has looked dormant to Google for seven months while 26 pages sat there waiting.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Install GA4, conversion tracking and call tracking. Before anything else and before any more pages. Within four weeks you would know what proportion of your twenty weekly enquiries the platform actually earns.
  • Put a number against the platform. Once you can see it, you can decide whether it is worth what you pay — and that is a decision you currently cannot make in either direction.
  • Add your website to your MCS register entry, and decide which phone number is the business number. Free, same week.
  • Add LocalBusiness and Service structured data across the 26 pages that already exist.
  • Decide what this business is selling. You are a nineteen-year-old commercial building contractor whose website never mentions building work. That is a positioning conversation before it is a marketing one, and it is worth more than every other item on this list.
  • Point the commercial pages harder — you prefer larger jobs and you already have seven commercial pages. With tracking on, they can be measured, improved and, if they work, multiplied.
  • Only then talk about more traffic. You do not have a traffic problem yet, and nobody can prove you do until step one is done.

Three or four jobs a week, from twenty conversations, and room for more of both.

You do not need more enquiries before you can tell which of the twenty are worth having.
