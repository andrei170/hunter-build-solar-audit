The Problem, In Your Own Numbers
The number this all comes back to

80 leads in six weeks produced 4 jobs. Roughly 20% answered the phone — about 16 of the 80. Of those 16 conversations, 4 became installs.

So the loss is not at the close. Sixty-four people never picked up at all. You call, leave voicemails, email, text — and you can see the messages have been read.

Your own verdict on the reporting: “£10 per lead is no good if they don't answer the phone.”

And it has got worse, not better

Since January you put the qualified-to-unqualified split at roughly 50/50. On the current campaign you said plainly it has “not been as good as the first campaign” — the first one, you were “snowballed with people that wanted to go ahead.”

The detail that matters most: on the earlier, better campaign you were paying around £35 a lead. You are now paying £10. The cheaper the lead got, the worse the business got. That is not a coincidence and it is the single most useful fact from the whole call.

You were also spending roughly double at that time, and were told the current campaign would “go a little bit easy to begin with.”

Who is actually filling the form in
  • Competitors. You had six last week that were “very obvious” rival installers changing panel sizes and inverters to price-check you.
  • People with no intent at all. One told you outright they were up on a night shift and bored.
  • Overnight traffic. A lot arrives after 7pm and the phone is “pinging all night.” You are reasonably not calling at that hour, so those leads are hours cold by morning.

Asked whether the hours could be restricted, your answer was: “They control all that. I don't have anything to do with that.”

And nothing is being recorded

You track follow-up manually — a log of who you called, emailed and texted, repeated for two or three days, then stopped. There is no CRM, no automated sequence, and no record that survives the week.

You have been pitched instant-callback systems repeatedly and rejected them because they are AI. “If I've had people following me, and it's AI receptionists, I'll just put the phone down.” Noted, and agreed with. Nothing we propose involves an AI voice answering your customers.

What Is Working — And This Matters
This is not a case for firing anybody

That channel has turned over close to £800,000 since February. You said so yourself and you were right to. A channel producing £800k is not a failed channel.

It has degraded. The question for the next call is when it degraded and why — not whether to scrap it. The creative was filmed eight weeks before we spoke, the decline covers the last six, and new material was shot in Corfu and was due to go live the week after our call. That timing is worth checking before any conclusion is drawn.

Your own position was that you are not removing anyone yet: “I'm still probably going to carry on with the video ads for the time being.” Nothing here asks you to change that.

Your SEO is also not the problem

£700/mo is producing 10–12 enquiries a month. Against £3,000/mo producing 4 real ones in six weeks, it is comfortably your most efficient channel — and it is only two months old, which is early for search. Search enquiries also arrive with intent by definition; nobody idly Googles “solar installer Bury St Edmunds” at 2am out of boredom.

You have 26 town-and-service pages across seven towns. That is better structure than any nearby competitor we were able to measure.

The Two Diagnoses That Explain The Rest
One — the form is long, but it does not qualify

Your form asks for a lot: name, address, yearly usage, internal or external, best time to call. People fill it all in anyway. Length is not qualification. A bored night-shift worker will type an address as readily as a buyer will.

What it does not ask is the thing that separates a 12-panel enquiry from an 80-panel one: whether they own the property, roof size and orientation, current bill, and when they actually want it done. Those are questions a real buyer can answer in seconds and a time-waster cannot be bothered to.

That also enables lead scoring — rank one to four, and spend your mornings on the fours instead of working the list in the order it arrived.

Two — the pixel is being taught to find the wrong people

Meta optimises toward whatever event you tell it is a success. At the moment that event is a submitted form. So it has spent since January learning what a person who submits this form looks like — and those people are, on your own numbers, about half time-wasters.

It is doing exactly what it was asked to. It was asked for the wrong thing. That is also why doubling the spend would not have fixed it — it would have bought more of the same people, faster, which is what happened last time when you spent double and got “a lot more jobs that qualified, but a lot more tyre kickers again as well.”

The Arithmetic, Using Only Your Figures
This is arithmetic, not a forecast

Of 80 leads, 16 answered and 4 bought. So of the people you actually got to speak to, roughly one in four bought.

The sixteen is the number worth moving, because the one-in-four is already respectable. If the same 80 leads produced 32 conversations instead of 16, and that one-in-four held, that is 8 jobs rather than 4.

At your stated £2,500–£3,500 profit per install, four additional installs across a six-week window is £10,000–£14,000 of profit from the leads you have already paid for.

We are not promising that. Answer rates depend on things neither of us controls. It is shown to establish one point only: the ceiling on this business right now is not lead volume, and more spend is not the first lever.

The ceiling nobody has mentioned to you

You said you need a day to a day and a half surveying per job, and that you are the one who surveys. Five installs a week implies five to seven and a half days of surveying inside a five-day week.

So five a week may be a capacity problem before it is a lead problem. You already identified this yourself — “probably just we need more people” — and then said the priority was more jobs. Both can be true, but if the surveying bottleneck is not addressed, additional qualified leads will queue rather than convert.

Worth deciding before spend changes: does someone else survey, or do the leads get filtered hard enough that you only drive to the ones worth driving to? The second is cheaper and is what everything here is built toward.

The Thing We Are Not Going To Repeat
TNW

Two years ago you paid £12,000 upfront for what was sold as a complete in-house acquisition system — trained callers, leads booked straight into your calendar, never pay for a lead again. You never heard from them again. They moved to Dubai and traded from there. You never got the money back, and you still hold the contract. Their setters recently rang you to pitch again.

That is the single most important piece of context from the call, and it is the reason the pricing page is structured the way it is. There is no large upfront fee, and nothing is sold as a system you operate yourself.

It also explains something about your own customers that is worth using: you have personally been burned by a confident pitch. So have the homeowners deciding whether to spend £15,000 with you. That is the most under-used asset in your marketing and it costs nothing to deploy.
